MCert exists to bring clarity and coordination to a rapidly growing youth digital mental health space. This overview reflects our north star, the stakeholders we serve, our approach to quality standards, and the path to sustainable impact.

01 Who We Are

Allow Us to Introduce Ourselves

MCert is a non-partisan organization that both defines evidence-informed Quality Standards for subclinical digital youth mental health tools and coordinates key stakeholders needed to drive their adoption and implementation in real-world settings.

We Are

  • A science-backed standard-setter that develops, maintains, and continuously updates quality standards as the field evolves.
  • A co-created framework that centers BIPOC, LGBTQIA+, and disabled youth in defining quality and in determining which tools are resourced.
  • A non-partisan coordinating layer that aligns funders, policymakers, providers, and innovators around a shared definition of quality.
  • An open-source resource hub that translates research into accessible tools and literacy materials for all stakeholders.
  • An implementation support partner that helps organizations successfully adopt tools in real-world settings.
  • A mechanism for connection to care by making high-quality, evidence-aligned tools easier to identify, fund, and integrate.

We Are Not

  • A certification body that approves or disapproves individual digital mental health tools or interventions.
  • A government regulator or enforcement agency that creates or mandates compliance requirements.
  • A consumer rating system or product review organization that ranks or scores and then lists individual apps or tools.
  • A provider of clinical care, therapeutic service, or a substitute for human connection, clinical judgment, or treatment.
  • A funding body or gatekeeper that allocates capital or determines which organizations receive investment or grants.
  • A partisan or industry-aligned organization that is funded by, or beholden to, any single sector or commercial interest.
02 Our Approach

Our North Star

Become the trusted standard that enables funders, government, and health systems to identify, fund, and scale high-quality subclinical digital mental health tools for youth, grounded in how young people actually experience value.

1

Industry-wide shared definitions

We help the entire ecosystem answer the foundational question: "What does 'good' mean for digital, subclinical mental health tools, including real-world implementation practices?" We will help answer this question iteratively as new innovations surface.

2

Measurable, youth-anchored standards

Defining and proving quality requires balancing safety, risk, outcomes, and engagement through measurable results. Those results must be anchored in how youth actually experience value, and be flexible enough to keep up with rapidly evolving technology.

3

A deliberate stakeholder sequence

We are intentionally sequencing the stakeholders we serve to systematically build proof and trust that result in both deep impact and a sustainable business model.

4

Create a cross-sector movement

We want to create a broader movement around these definitions of quality that drives long-term awareness, trust, and a shift towards transparency and clarity.

5

Equity is built into everything we do

We will explicitly name, prioritize, and operationalize equity for BIPOC, LGBTQIA+, and disabled youth so that the communities most impacted by the mental health crisis are centered in how quality is defined, who evaluates it, and which tools get resourced.

6

Open literacy and targeted support

MCert makes its knowledge, research, and quality standards freely available to all stakeholders. For system enablers like funders, health systems, and government agencies, MCert goes further, offering research, implementation guidance, and consulting from RFP design through deployment and evaluation.

03 Who We Serve

Partnering for Impact: Cultural Fit First. Proof Second. Scale Third.

We have designed a deliberate, phased approach to trust-building that starts with the most culturally aligned partners and lets each stage generate the proof, credibility, and revenue needed to bring in the next. This is not a growth strategy. It is a legitimacy strategy — because legitimacy is what helps standards get adopted and brings the most benefit to the ecosystem. At every stage, the ultimate beneficiary is the same: youth.

Phase 1

Philanthropic Funders Where the market signal begins

Philanthropic capital is uniquely suited to move first because it is structurally designed to act before the evidence is complete. MCert gives funders exactly what they need to take that first credible bet: strong signals of need and support from key ecosystem leaders who have shaped our framework and are partnering with us on sustainable adoption.

Without clear standards, philanthropic dollars flow toward tools that are popular rather than proven. MCert gives funders the language and criteria to allocate capital with confidence, and to defend funding decisions with quantitative impact data.

When a respected funder adopts MCert's standards, it signals to the market that those standards are credible and worth following, laying the foundation for broader stakeholder adoption.

Phase 2

Government Health & Education The bridge between credibility and scale

Governmental bodies across Healthcare and Education share MCert's public-interest orientation and have a clear mandate to serve youth and communities. With proof points from stage one, they have both the evidence and institutional justification to align with our standards. The cultural leap is small; the potential impact is large.

What MCert offers government partners is a nonpartisan infrastructure that withstands public scrutiny, legislative review, and the accountability demands of public resources. In return, government adoption provides something philanthropy alone cannot: official recognition, signaling to the broader market that MCert's standards are not just credible, but sanctioned.

Contracts and larger-scale pilots also introduce stable, recurring revenue that reduces philanthropic dependence and gives MCert the durability to operate at the scale the problem demands.

Phase 3

Private Payors & Health Systems Where standards become infrastructure

Payors are the hardest to move; their culture is built around risk management, reimbursement cycles, and demonstrated ROI. Engaging them too early risks rejection and can set the broader standards movement back. They belong third, not because they matter less, but because what they require takes time and sequencing to build.

By the time MCert engages payors at scale, that work will be done. MCert arrives at the payor table with proof and a track record, making adoption a defensible decision rather than a leap of faith.

When private health systems incorporate MCert's standards into procurement and reimbursement decisions, those standards stop being a framework and become infrastructure — embedded in the financial architecture of how the health system evaluates, pays for, and scales these tools.

04 Ecosystem Map

MCert Starts Where Decisions Are Made

MCert's Quality Standards enable philanthropy, government, and payors to identify and fund digital mental health tools that actually work for youth. That shared definition of "good" cascades through tool developers, state agencies, and health systems, then reaches young people through schools, nonprofits, caregivers, peers, and clinicians. Youth feedback closes the loop — so both our Quality Standards and the decisions built on them keep getting stronger.

05 Partnerships

Filling the Gap, Not Duplicating the Work

The youth mental health ecosystem is full of organizations doing vital work. MCert is not here to duplicate that work. We are here to fill the remaining gaps and, in doing so, become a force multiplier for the partners who need it most.

Digital Health Literacy

By partnering with organizations focused on health literacy and public education, MCert helps ensure that a shared understanding of quality spreads across the entire ecosystem. We are committed to making our science-backed definition of quality a true public good: open, accessible, and actionable for everyone, from senior health system leaders to families navigating options for their kids.

Research & Evaluation

Rigorous, independent research is the backbone of credible standards. MCert partners with leading academic institutions and research organizations to ground our Quality Standards in evidence, contribute to the growing body of knowledge on what actually works in subclinical digital mental health, and ensure our framework evolves alongside the science.

Adoption & Implementation

Standards only work if they are tested in the real world. MCert will partner with government and healthcare entities, accelerators, youth-focused programs, and digital health innovators to pilot our Quality Standards with existing and emerging tools, creating a feedback loop that strengthens both the framework and the products being evaluated.

Policy & Advocacy

Across the country, organizations, nonprofits, parent-led coalitions, and community advocates are working to direct resources toward youth mental health. MCert wants to be their partner, offering a shared, trusted quality framework to ensure that, however funds are allocated, they go toward solutions that actually work for young people.

Science Partner Spotlight

Fit Minded

Founded by Dr. Jen Huberty, former Head of Science at Calm

Fit Minded is a strategic applied science firm bringing the scientific rigor MCert's Quality Standards require. As MCert's Science Chair, Dr. Huberty leads our Advisory Board's science workstream and developed a 35-page literature review and working paper that forms the backbone of our Shared Quality Standards framework.

Each topic in that paper is organized around a specific need raised by convening attendees and grounded in research-backed responses. Together, Fit Minded and MCert are building the first scientific framework for evaluating subclinical digital mental health tools, balancing innovation with safety and ensuring our standards evolve alongside the science.

06 Business Model

Sustainable by Design. Structured to Scale.

We evaluated eight business model options for MCert across financial access, governance equity, sustainability, and other dimensions. The two most viable near-term models are outlined below.

Now — 24 Months

Project & Research Funding

Grant and philanthropic funding gives MCert the flexibility to validate our Quality Standards, work directly with funders to meet their needs, and build relationships with local and state governments. This early stage lets us do the work and build the proof before earned revenue is required.

2 — 5 Years

Nonprofit with Earned Revenue

As MCert's standards gain credibility, earned revenue through a nonprofit structure becomes our sustainable model. We begin earning revenue by serving government entities first, consulting and advising on the evaluation and implementation of digital mental health solutions for youth.

As federal oversight in healthcare recedes, states and localities are not just filling the gap. They are becoming the decision-makers, drawing on two types of funds to support youth: legislative appropriations and settlements. The funds exist. What is missing is a trusted, youth-informed standard to help deploy money toward solutions that work.

New Mexico illustrates both pools of capital in action. In 2025, the state passed a landmark behavioral health reform package that included a $1 billion Behavioral Health Trust Fund and $200 million in infrastructure appropriations. At the same time, New Mexico won a landmark $375 million verdict against Meta for harms caused to youth. Because those settlement dollars are not taxpayer funds, they carry far fewer bureaucratic constraints and can be redirected toward the very solutions that address the harms that generated them.

42 states and counting have filed similar lawsuits against social media companies, making this a national pattern, not a single state story. MCert is already active in New Mexico — advising the state on how to allocate funds for youth mental health, co-designing summits that bring youth voices into policy, and testifying before the New Mexico State Senate on social media protection bills for youth.
